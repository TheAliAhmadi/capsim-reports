Quick glossary (first-use Capsim terms): An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy you in the sales channel (mainly sales budget, shared across products). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band. SG&A is selling, general & administrative expense (promotion, sales, admin). Net plant improvements on the cash flow statement are negative when you buy capacity/automation and positive when you sell capacity for cash. Plant utilization above 100% means a second shift: more output, but at higher labor cost.

  1. 1. Cash buffer — a quiet strength. Ending cash of $3,765 and no emergency loan give you room to invest. Keep matching plant and inventory spending to financing.
  2. 2. Inventory tied up (or freed) meaningful cash. Ending inventory on the balance sheet is $16,453; on the cash flow statement, inventory freed up about $29,051 of cash this year (stock drawn down). Products with large unsold stacks: Baker (323 units), Bold (207 units), Buddy (295 units). Selling down old stock is a great way to free cash, so keep that habit and plan production from realistic demand.
  3. 3. You stocked out where customers wanted more — a good problem, if you respond. Stockouts on Bead mean demand exceeded supply. Raise production (and check capacity), and fund accessibility so you do not leave sales on the table.
  4. 4. You right-sized the plant, which turned idle capacity into cash. You sold plant capacity, which brought in about $27,430 of cash. Capsim also recorded a one-time accounting gain of about $16,177 on that sale. It helped this year’s bottom line, so the underlying operating improvement is real but a little smaller than the headline profit suggests. Together with operating cash of $17,895, that let you pay down debt (about $0 of long-term debt retired early) and still finish the year with $3,765 in the bank. The trade-off: less capacity means less room to grow, so watch utilization (and second-shift costs) if demand picks up.

Bright spot: a profitable Round 7. Protect that edge with disciplined cash and inventory.

The through-line: you showed you can make money in the graded simulation. Round 8 is about building on that momentum — tighten positioning, clear excess inventory, and keep investing where customers already reward you.
