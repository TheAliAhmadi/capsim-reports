Quick glossary (first-use Capsim terms): An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy you in the sales channel (mainly sales budget, shared across products). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band. SG&A is selling, general & administrative expense (promotion, sales, admin). Net plant improvements on the cash flow statement are negative when you buy capacity/automation and positive when you sell capacity for cash. Plant utilization above 100% means a second shift: more output, but at higher labor cost.

  1. 1. Cash buffer — a quiet strength. Ending cash of $6,098 and no emergency loan give you room to invest. Keep matching plant and inventory spending to financing.
  2. 2. Inventory tied up (or freed) meaningful cash. Ending inventory on the balance sheet is $20,901; on the cash flow statement, inventory absorbed about $2,684 of cash this year (stock built up). Round 4 fix: price inside the band for the segment you are in, or reposition toward a segment where that price can work. Do not schedule another large run until the match is coherent.
  4. 4. You stocked out where customers wanted more — a good problem, if you respond. Stockouts on Bead mean demand exceeded supply. Raise production (and check capacity), and fund accessibility so you do not leave sales on the table.
  5. 5. Margin and overhead need a closer handshake. Company CM 32.3% and SG&A-to-sales 19.2%. Pair marketing budgets with product fixes — not instead of them.
  6. 6. You right-sized the plant, which turned idle capacity into cash. You sold plant capacity, which brought in about $10,270 of cash. Capsim also recorded a one-time accounting gain of about $2,710 on that sale. The trade-off: less capacity means less room to grow, so watch utilization (and second-shift costs) if demand picks up.
The through-line: you turned a Round 3 cash crisis into a Round 4 cash buffer. Now convert that discipline into finer product fit and healthier margins so the annual P&L can catch up.
