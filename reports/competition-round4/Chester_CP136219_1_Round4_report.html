Quick glossary (first-use Capsim terms): An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy you in the sales channel (mainly sales budget, shared across products). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band. SG&A is selling, general & administrative expense (promotion, sales, admin). Net plant improvements on the cash flow statement are negative when you buy capacity/automation and positive when you sell capacity for cash. Plant utilization above 100% means a second shift: more output, but at higher labor cost.

  1. 1. Cash buffer — a quiet strength. Ending cash of $15,293 and no emergency loan give you room to invest. Keep matching plant and inventory spending to financing.
  2. 2. Inventory tied up (or freed) meaningful cash. Ending inventory on the balance sheet is $22,563; on the cash flow statement, inventory absorbed about $15,999 of cash this year (stock built up). Products with large unsold stacks: Cake (387 units), Creak (248 units), Cid (266 units). Unsold units are cash sitting on a shelf. Align next round’s production with realistic demand.
  3. 3. You stocked out where customers wanted more — a good problem, if you respond. Stockouts on Cedar mean demand exceeded supply. Raise production (and check capacity), and fund accessibility so you do not leave sales on the table.
  4. 4. You invested in the plant, so finance it on purpose. You bought about $27,080 of new plant (capacity and/or automation) (stock issued $0, long-term debt issued $19,800). Keep the financing side as intentional as the spending side.

Bright spots to build on: Cake in Traditional (customer survey 54), Cedar in Low End (customer survey 65), Creak in High End (customer survey 40). Keep funding what already resonates.

The through-line: you showed you can make money in the graded simulation. Round 5 is about widening the lead — tighten positioning, clear excess inventory, and keep investing where customers already reward you.
