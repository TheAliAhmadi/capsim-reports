    Adjusted Leaderboard · You vs. your own industry

    Every team spent the semester competing only against the other firms in its own industry. Different industries had different rivals and market conditions, so this view ranks each team by how it did against its own industry rivals, round by round.

    It sits beside the official leaderboard rather than replacing it. Think of it as a second way to see strong play: a team that kept beating tough rivals shows up here even if its raw total sits lower on the main board.

    How this is calculated
    1. Each round, compare with your industry. In every competition round (1 to 8) we look at your Balanced Scorecard (BSC) and count how many of your industry rivals you outscored. A rival with exactly the same score counts as half. Divide by the number of rivals (6 or 7) to get your round percentile: 100% means you beat every rival that round.
    2. Average the eight rounds. Your Adjusted score is the average of those eight round percentiles. That's the "outscored X% of your industry rivals each round" number.
    3. Break ties with margin. If two teams have the same average, the one that beat its rivals by bigger margins goes first. That margin is the average number of industry standard deviations above the industry mean each round, shown as "+1.13 SD".
    4. #1 rounds. These are the rounds where a team had the top BSC in its industry. A shared top score counts for every tied team.

    One honest caveat: a within-industry rank can't tell apart strong opponents from a tough market, so this view sits alongside the official Balanced Scorecard ranking rather than replacing it.
